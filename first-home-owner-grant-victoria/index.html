What is the First Home Owner Grant in Victoria?

The First Home Owner Grant (FHOG) is a $10,000 payment from the Victorian Government to help eligible first home buyers buy or build a new home in Victoria to live in. It applies to newly constructed or never-occupied properties valued up to $750,000. Eligibility applies — the conditions are set by the State Revenue Office, not by us or your lender.

The catch that surprises most people: it is a new-build grant only. If you buy an established home — a 1970s brick veneer in Morwell, a weatherboard in Moe — there is no grant, no matter how much of a first home buyer you are. That single rule decides whether the $10,000 is on the table for you.

That makes the FHOG unusually relevant across Gippsland, because a large share of the region’s new stock is exactly what the grant is written for: house-and-land in the growth estates around Traralgon, Warragul, Drouin and Sale, and knock-down rebuilds through the older Latrobe Valley streets.

Does my property count as a “new home”?

The State Revenue Office defines a new home as one that has not previously been sold, occupied as a home, leased out, or used for short-term accommodation. In practice that covers:

  • House and land packages — the most common FHOG purchase in Gippsland.
  • Building on land you already own — a fixed-price build contract with a registered builder.
  • A brand-new home bought from a builder or developer, provided nobody has lived in it, rented it, or listed it as short-stay accommodation.
  • Substantially renovated homes, and homes built to replace a demolished one (a knock-down rebuild).

What does not count: an established home, an investment property you will not live in, or a “new” home that has already been tenanted.

It stacks — and that is where the real money is

The FHOG is rarely the only benefit a Gippsland first home buyer can access. Used together, the effect is much larger than $10,000:

  • Stamp duty — Victorian first home buyers pay no land transfer duty up to $600,000, and a reduced amount from $600,001 to $750,000, where the home will be their principal place of residence. Run your own figure on our stamp duty calculator.
  • A low-deposit guarantee — eligible first home buyers can buy with a small deposit and no lenders mortgage insurance under the Australian Government 5% Deposit Scheme (previously called the First Home Guarantee). See our guide to the scheme in Gippsland.
  • Your deposit itself — what you actually need up front is smaller than most people assume. Our deposit guide walks through it.

Eligibility applies to each of these separately, and qualifying for one does not automatically qualify you for another. Working out which ones you actually land is a large part of what we do on a first appointment.

How you apply

Most buyers never lodge the paperwork themselves. If your lender is an approved agent, the grant is applied for through your home loan application and paid at settlement for a purchase, or at the first progress payment for a build. Otherwise you apply directly to the State Revenue Office after settlement or completion.

The practical point is timing. On a build, the grant lands part-way through construction rather than up front, so it changes what you need in the bank on day one but not what you need to start. Getting that sequence right — deposit, land settlement, construction drawdowns, grant — is where a broker earns their keep on a new build. Our construction loan page covers how the drawdowns work.

Where this matters in Gippsland

New-build activity in the region is concentrated in a handful of places, and they are exactly where the FHOG bites: the estates on the edges of Traralgon, the west Gippsland growth corridor through Warragul and Drouin, and new land releases around Sale and Moe. Regional Victorian prices also mean far more of this stock sits under the $750,000 cap than it would in Melbourne, so the grant is live for a much larger share of local buyers.

The honest answer on whether to chase it

$10,000 is real money, but it should not be the reason you build. A new build can cost more per square metre than an established home, takes longer, and carries construction risk an established purchase does not. If a new build genuinely suits you, the grant is a strong reason to make it your first home rather than your second. If it does not, chasing $10,000 into the wrong purchase is an expensive way to save money. Which one applies depends on your situation, and it is worth talking through properly.

First Home Owner Grant questions, answered

How much is the First Home Owner Grant in Victoria?

$10,000, for eligible first home buyers buying or building a new home in Victoria to live in, valued up to $750,000. Eligibility applies.

Can I get the grant on an established home?

No. The Victorian grant is for newly constructed or never-occupied homes only. An established home does not qualify — though you may still qualify for the first home buyer stamp duty exemption or concession, and for a low-deposit guarantee.

Does a knock-down rebuild count?

The State Revenue Office includes homes built to replace a demolished home, and substantially renovated homes, in its definition of a new home. The detail matters, so it is worth confirming your specific build before you commit.

Do I have to live in the home?

Yes. At least one applicant must live in it as their principal place of residence for at least 12 months, starting within 12 months of settlement or completion of construction. It is waived for certain Australian Defence Force personnel.

When do I actually receive the money?

If your lender is an approved agent, it is applied for with your loan and paid at settlement for a purchase, or at the first progress payment for a build. Otherwise you apply to the State Revenue Office after settlement or completion.

Can I get the grant and the stamp duty exemption?

They are separate benefits with separate rules, and many buyers qualify for both. Victorian first home buyers pay no duty up to $600,000 and a reduced amount to $750,000 on their principal place of residence. We check which ones you land before you sign anything.

Getting a Loan with Money Sense Lending

1. Understanding Your Needs

Your mortgage journey begins with a detailed consultation with your Finance & Mortgage Broker. Your broker will listen to your property goals, whether you’re buying a home, investing in real estate, or seeking a commercial loan. They will assess your financial situation, considering factors like income, savings, liabilities, and credit history, to provide tailored advice on loan options available to you.

2. Financial Positioning

The next step involves your broker conducting a thorough evaluation of your financial position. This includes reviewing your income, bank statements, assets, liabilities, credit history, and savings. Your broker will then calculate your borrowing capacity, helping you understand how much you can afford to borrow and how much your monthly repayments might be. Your broker will also assess whether you qualify for any interest rate discounts or government schemes, like first-home buyer grants

3. Comparing Loan Options

Once your financial situation is fully assessed, your broker will begin comparing loan products from a range of lenders across Australia. They will help you weigh the benefits of fixed interest rate loans versus variable interest rate loans, as well as exploring options for offset accounts and interest rate discounts. They will also explain important terms like loan features, fees, and other considerations like the potential for future rate changes or LVR adjustments.

4. Pre-Approval Process

Pre-approval is a crucial step in the property buying process. With pre-approval, you’ll have a clear idea of the loan amount you can borrow, which strengthens your position in the local property market. Your broker will help you gather the necessary documents and submit them to the lender for pre-approval, ensuring the process is as quick and straightforward as possible.

5. Submitting the Loan Application

Once pre-approval is in hand, your broker will help you complete the loan application. They will guide you through submitting all necessary paperwork to the lender, including documents like bank statements, proof of income, and details of existing liabilities. Your broker will communicate directly with the lender to ensure the application is processed quickly, ensuring no delays in getting you the loan you need.

6. Loan Approval & Settlement

After your loan is approved, your broker will review the final loan offer with you. They will also work with you to ensure you understand the terms of the loan and guide you through the settlement process. Your broker will remain available to help you navigate any final hurdles before you complete the purchase of your property.

7. Finalising Ownership

The final step is settlement, when your loan is officially advanced, and you take ownership of the property. Your broker will coordinate with the lender and conveyancer to ensure the transfer goes smoothly. Once the settlement is complete, you’ll officially become the owner of the property, and your Finance & Mortgage Broker will continue to offer guidance to help you manage your loan and stay on track with repayments.
