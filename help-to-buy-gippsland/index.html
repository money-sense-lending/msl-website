What is Help to Buy — and does it work in Gippsland?

Help to Buy is the Australian Government’s shared equity scheme. You buy the home with as little as a 2% deposit, and the government contributes part of the purchase price in exchange for owning that share of the property. It is a genuinely different animal to the low-deposit guarantees most first home buyers hear about, and the trade-off is real — which is exactly why it is worth understanding before you chase it. Eligibility applies to everything below.

How the shared equity part actually works

Under Help to Buy the government contributes up to 30% of the purchase price for an existing home, or up to 40% for a new home. You take a normal home loan for the rest, and because your loan is smaller relative to the property value, you avoid lenders mortgage insurance. You live in the home as your principal place of residence — it is not an investment play.

The part people skim past: the government keeps that share. When you sell, or when you buy the share back, the government’s portion is settled at its share of the value at that time. If the home has grown in value, the government’s slice has grown with it. You are trading a piece of your future capital growth for the ability to get in now. For some Gippsland buyers that is a very good deal. For others it is not, and a plain 5% deposit is the better path.

The catch nobody mentions: only two lenders currently offer it

Help to Buy is delivered through participating lenders, and you cannot apply to Housing Australia directly. At the moment only two lenders are approved to offer it, with more expected to join during 2026. That matters more than it sounds. A scheme is only as useful as the lender behind it — if their credit policy does not suit your income type, your deposit source or your employment history, the scheme is academic.

This is the honest reason we do not push Help to Buy at every first home buyer who walks into the Church Street office. We work across a panel of 30+ lenders, and for a lot of Gippsland buyers the 5% Deposit Scheme through a lender whose policy actually fits gets them into the same house, owning all of it, without an equity share to unwind later.

Who Help to Buy genuinely suits

  • You are under the income caps and your borrowing power is the wall. If a normal loan will not stretch to the homes you want, a government equity share can bridge a gap that saving harder simply cannot.
  • You have a small deposit and rent is eating the savings. Two per cent is a materially lower bar than five, and every year of waiting has its own cost.
  • You plan to stay put for a long time. Shared equity punishes short holds. The longer you own, the more room you have to buy the share back on your terms.

Who it does not suit

  • Buyers close to the income caps — a pay rise after settlement can trigger a requirement to start buying back the government’s share. Worth planning for before you sign, not after.
  • Anyone who wants clean, outright ownership. That is a legitimate preference, not a financial mistake.
  • Buyers who qualify comfortably under the 5% Deposit Scheme. If you can get there with 5% and no equity share, that is usually the stronger position.

Help to Buy questions, answered

Do I own the whole house under Help to Buy?

No — that is the core trade-off. The government contributes up to 30% of the price for an existing home, or up to 40% for a new build, and it keeps that share until you buy it back or sell. You live in the home as your own, but you do not own all of it. Eligibility applies.

What is the Help to Buy price cap in Gippsland?

$650,000. Victoria has two caps — $950,000 for Melbourne and Geelong, and $650,000 for the rest of the state, which covers every Gippsland town. The same $650,000 cap applies to the Australian Government 5% Deposit Scheme.

What are the Help to Buy income limits?

For the 2026–27 financial year the caps are $103,000 for a single applicant and $165,000 for joint applicants or a single parent. They are taxable-income figures and are reviewed annually. If you are near the line, check before you apply — we can do that in one chat.

Can any lender do Help to Buy?

No. It runs through participating lenders only, and you cannot apply to Housing Australia directly. At present just two lenders are approved to offer it, with the panel expected to expand during 2026. That is a genuine constraint — a scheme only helps if that lender’s credit policy suits your situation.

Is Help to Buy better than the 5% Deposit Scheme?

It depends entirely on your situation. Help to Buy needs a smaller deposit, but the government keeps an equity share in your home. The 5% Deposit Scheme has no income cap, no place limit, and leaves you owning all of the property. If you qualify comfortably under the 5% scheme, that is usually the stronger position — we will show you the maths either way.

Getting a Loan with Money Sense Lending

1. Understanding Your Needs

Your mortgage journey begins with a detailed consultation with your Finance & Mortgage Broker. Your broker will listen to your property goals, whether you’re buying a home, investing in real estate, or seeking a commercial loan. They will assess your financial situation, considering factors like income, savings, liabilities, and credit history, to provide tailored advice on loan options available to you.

2. Financial Positioning

The next step involves your broker conducting a thorough evaluation of your financial position. This includes reviewing your income, bank statements, assets, liabilities, credit history, and savings. Your broker will then calculate your borrowing capacity, helping you understand how much you can afford to borrow and how much your monthly repayments might be. Your broker will also assess whether you qualify for any interest rate discounts or government schemes, like first-home buyer grants

3. Comparing Loan Options

Once your financial situation is fully assessed, your broker will begin comparing loan products from a range of lenders across Australia. They will help you weigh the benefits of fixed interest rate loans versus variable interest rate loans, as well as exploring options for offset accounts and interest rate discounts. They will also explain important terms like loan features, fees, and other considerations like the potential for future rate changes or LVR adjustments.

4. Pre-Approval Process

Pre-approval is a crucial step in the property buying process. With pre-approval, you’ll have a clear idea of the loan amount you can borrow, which strengthens your position in the local property market. Your broker will help you gather the necessary documents and submit them to the lender for pre-approval, ensuring the process is as quick and straightforward as possible.

5. Submitting the Loan Application

Once pre-approval is in hand, your broker will help you complete the loan application. They will guide you through submitting all necessary paperwork to the lender, including documents like bank statements, proof of income, and details of existing liabilities. Your broker will communicate directly with the lender to ensure the application is processed quickly, ensuring no delays in getting you the loan you need.

6. Loan Approval & Settlement

After your loan is approved, your broker will review the final loan offer with you. They will also work with you to ensure you understand the terms of the loan and guide you through the settlement process. Your broker will remain available to help you navigate any final hurdles before you complete the purchase of your property.

7. Finalising Ownership

The final step is settlement, when your loan is officially advanced, and you take ownership of the property. Your broker will coordinate with the lender and conveyancer to ensure the transfer goes smoothly. Once the settlement is complete, you’ll officially become the owner of the property, and your Finance & Mortgage Broker will continue to offer guidance to help you manage your loan and stay on track with repayments.
